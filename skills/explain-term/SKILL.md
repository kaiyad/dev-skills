---

Take a single technical term and leave the user able to define it in their own words, picture it, and recognize when it applies. Follow `../../references/explain-visually.md` for the ELI5 rules and the visual.

## Steps

1. **One-line ELI5 first.** A plain-language sentence, then one everyday analogy that genuinely maps onto the term — and name the single place the analogy breaks, so it isn't over-trusted.
2. **Build from the ground up.** Define the term with no undefined jargon. If it depends on a prerequisite concept, introduce that first; don't explain a word with harder words.
3. **Draw the one picture.** A diagram that shows what the term means *mechanically* — not a logo, a mechanism (`explain-visually.md` picks the form). For something layered or interactive, an HTML artifact.
4. **Work at least one example in detail.** A concrete, traceable case showing the term in action, stepped through — not just named. Then a **contrasting non-example**: a case that looks similar but *isn't* the thing, to draw the boundary sharply. The pair teaches the edge, which is where understanding actually lives.
5. **Say when you'd reach for it.** The real situations the term shows up in, so it becomes a tool the user can recognize the need for, not just a word they can recite.
6. **Depth on demand.** Lead with the ELI5 and the picture; put the precise, technical definition and the caveats below, for the reader who wants to go further.

## Completion

The reader can define the term in their own words, point at the picture and say what it shows, and name a situation where it applies and one where it doesn't. The analogy's limit is stated; at least one worked example and one contrasting non-example are present.
